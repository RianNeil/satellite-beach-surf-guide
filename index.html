How we rate

Poor, Fair, and Good are estimates for an east-facing Space Coast beach. They are not a report from the water.

The compass label is the nearest of 16 points. Southwest, west-southwest, and west count as offshore. Northeast, east-northeast, and east count as onshore. Any other point counts as cross-shore. A rounded speed of 0 mph is calm, and calm ignores direction.

  • Wave height: 2 points from 2 to 5 ft. 1 point from 1.5 ft up to 2 ft, or from just over 5 ft through 7 ft. 0 points otherwise.
  • Period: the swell period when the model has one, otherwise the combined wave period. A reported period of 0 seconds is treated as missing. 2 points at 10 seconds or longer. 1 point from 8 seconds up to 10. 0 points under 8 seconds.
  • Wind: offshore scores 2 points at 18 mph or less and 1 point if stronger. Onshore scores 0 points at 12 mph or more and 1 point if lighter. Cross-shore scores 1 point at 18 mph or less and 0 if stronger. Calm scores 1 point.

Add the three scores. 5 or 6 is Good. 3 or 4 is Fair. 2 or less is Poor. If the wave-height score is 0 (under 1.5 ft or over 7 ft), the label stops at Fair.

Before scoring, wave height and period are rounded to the nearest tenth, speed to the nearest mile per hour, and direction to the nearest degree. On each card, the estimate uses the current wave height, period, and wind. In the five-day table, wave height is the highest hour that day, period is the period in that same hour, and wind is the average from 7:00 a.m. through 6:00 p.m. ET. That speed is the mean of those hours. The direction is the speed-weighted average.

These numbers are model forecasts and estimates, not measurements on the beach, and they are not a substitute for checking the conditions yourself. Waves and swell come from the NOAA GFS Wave model (0.16°) through Open-Meteo, at the nearest sea cell, a few miles offshore. Wind is an Open-Meteo forecast at each beach. Water temperature, when shown, is the model sea-surface temperature. Tide predictions are for the Port Canaveral station, not the beach.

Official sources

Cross-check the model: NDBC buoy 41113 (Canaveral Nearshore), NDBC buoy 41009 (Canaveral East), NWS Melbourne, and NOAA tides at Port Canaveral (Trident Pier).
