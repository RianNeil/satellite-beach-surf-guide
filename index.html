How we rate

Each beach gets a whole-number score from 0 to 10 for the level you pick: Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced. The same waves can score very differently. This is a model estimate, not a report from the water, and it is not a reason to ignore the flags or a lifeguard.

The score uses the GFS offshore wave height and period, the swell direction against that beach, and the wind. Effective wind is the greater of the speed and three-quarters of the gust, and chop counts from the first mile per hour. Wind direction is a continuous angle from offshore, not a 16-point onshore/cross/offshore bucket. The shore-facing estimates, measured from these coordinates with the ocean to the east, are about 88° at Cocoa Beach, 78° at Satellite Beach, and 75° at Melbourne Beach. Tide only nudges the score, and that preference is low confidence. Gentle mode is optional and tightens the beginner range toward smaller surf.

Beginner scores stay capped while a rip risk is in effect: 3 when the risk is high, 6 when it is moderate. Intermediate scores drop by 1, and the card keeps a warning. Until the model separates swell from wind sea, intermediate scores stop at 8. Strong offshore wind caps a beginner score around 5, because paddling back in is hard. More than about 24 hours out, hazards may be unknown, and a beginner score will not read as a decision to paddle out. A tropical or hurricane swell is not scored. If the rip check does not come back, beginner scores stay hidden. Missing wave height or period shows “No estimate.”

The five-day table uses daylight hours only, 7:00 a.m. through 6:00 p.m. ET. For today it starts at the current hour, so an overnight peak is not the day’s wave. The score in that table is the best daytime hour for the level you picked.

These numbers are model forecasts and estimates, not measurements on the beach. The large wave number is the NOAA GFS Wave model (0.16°) through Open-Meteo, at the nearest sea cell. When GFS, MeteoFrance, and ECMWF WAM differ by more than about 40%, the card says so and shows the range. GFS stays the primary number. Wind now is the NOAA Trident Pier observation when it is fresh. Hourly wind is an Open-Meteo sea-side cell, and the page checks that the returned point actually moved east of the inland cell. Rip and surf hazards for all three beaches come from NWS zone FLZ747, including the Melbourne surf zone forecast. Cocoa Beach Pier’s marine zone is not used for rip alerts. Water temperature, when shown, is the model sea-surface temperature. Tide predictions are for Port Canaveral, not the beach.

Official sources

Cross-check the model: NDBC buoy 41113 (Canaveral Nearshore), NDBC buoy 41009 (Canaveral East), NWS Melbourne, and NOAA tides at Port Canaveral (Trident Pier).
